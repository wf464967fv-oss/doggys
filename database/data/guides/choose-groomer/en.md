Grooming is not just about looks. A well-kept coat protects the skin, prevents painful mats and makes it easier to spot a lump, a wound or parasites early. The key is finding the right groomer: someone who puts your dog at ease. Salon or mobile grooming? Here is how to choose.

## Does my dog need a groomer?

Every dog needs coat care, but not necessarily from a professional. It all depends on the type of coat.

- **Short coats** (Labrador, beagle, pug…): weekly brushing and the occasional bath are usually enough. A salon visit during moulting can help remove the undercoat.
- **Long or medium coats** (golden retriever, Australian shepherd, cavalier King Charles…): brushing several times a week and professional grooming roughly every 2 to 3 months.
- **Continuously growing coats** (poodle, bichon, shih tzu, Yorkshire terrier…): the hair does not shed and must be cut, usually every 4 to 8 weeks.
- **Wire coats** (schnauzer, wire-haired fox terrier, some dachshunds…): ideally maintained by **hand-stripping**, a specialist skill.

> **Good to know**: do not shave a double-coated dog (husky, German shepherd, golden retriever…) to “keep it cool” in summer. The undercoat insulates against both heat and cold, and clipping can permanently damage regrowth. Ask your groomer for advice.

## What a grooming session includes

A standard session usually includes:

- detangling and brushing;
- a bath with a suitable shampoo, followed by drying;
- a trim, clip or hand-strip depending on the breed and your wishes;
- nail clipping and ear cleaning;
- often, tidying the paw pads and the area around the eyes.

Prices vary with the size of the dog, the condition of the coat and the type of service. A badly matted dog takes longer, so regular upkeep often works out cheaper.

## Grooming salon or mobile groomer?

### The grooming salon

**Pros**: full equipment (baths, adjustable tables, powerful dryers), often wide opening hours and generally lower prices. For a sociable dog that is happy in the car, it is an excellent option.

**Things to consider**: the journey, possibly waiting in a kennel, the presence of other dogs and the noise of dryers. Some anxious dogs find this hard.

### Mobile grooming

This can take place in your home or in a **fitted grooming van** parked outside.

**Pros**: no journey, no other dogs, calm one-to-one time with the groomer. It is ideal for dogs that are **older, anxious, unwell or reactive**, and for families short on time or without transport.

**Things to consider**: prices are often a little higher, and availability can be limited in rural areas.

## How to spot a good groomer

Dog grooming is not a regulated profession in Belgium, and no diploma is required. So rely on concrete criteria.

### Good signs

- **They ask questions** about your dog: age, health, temperament, fears, past experiences.
- **The premises are clean**, tidy and odour-free, and dogs do not spend long in cages.
- **They are transparent** about prices and how the session will go.
- **They keep learning**, know breed standards and specific techniques (hand-stripping, scissoring).
- **They put welfare first**: breaks if the dog is stressed, no rough restraint, and they will refuse a style that would hurt a badly matted dog (a short clip is sometimes kinder).
- **They tell you what they noticed**: redness, a lump, an ear infection, parasites. They do not diagnose, but advise you to see your vet.

### Warning signs

- They refuse to let you see where and how your dog is kept.
- Your dog repeatedly comes back very stressed, injured or with significant irritation.
- The groomer offers sedatives themselves: sedation can only be decided on and given by a vet.

## Preparing for the first appointment

1. **Get your dog used to handling** from a young age: paws, ears, muzzle, brushing. Reward its patience.
2. **For a puppy**, a short, pleasant “getting to know you” visit at around 3 or 4 months, once its vaccinations are well under way, helps it link the salon with good experiences.
3. **Brush it beforehand** if you can, to reduce detangling time.
4. **Let it burn off energy** before the appointment: a dog that has had its walk is calmer.
5. **Tell the groomer** about any health problems, old age or behaviour issues (fear, biting).
6. **Keep goodbyes short**: emotional farewells make dogs anxious.

## Caring for the coat between visits

- **Brush regularly** with a tool suited to the coat (slicker brush, comb, grooming glove, rubber curry). Ask your groomer to show you the right technique.
- **Watch the mat-prone areas**: behind the ears, under the armpits, the backs of the thighs.
- **Check the nails**: if you can hear them clicking on tiles, they are probably too long.
- **Do not bathe too often**, and always use a dog shampoo, never a human product.
- **After a woodland walk**, use brushing time to check for ticks.

## Grooming an older or anxious dog

Dogs that are stiff, hard of hearing or easily panicked need a little extra thought. Stick with one groomer your dog knows, book shorter sessions and ask whether breaks are possible. Mobile grooming is often the calmest choice here. Mention arthritis, heart problems or medication beforehand, so the groomer can adapt positions and drying time.

> **Good to know**: many groomers offer packages for puppies, older dogs or sensitive dogs, with shorter sessions. Do not hesitate to ask.

## In short

Salon or mobile, the best groomer respects your dog’s pace, explains what they are doing and tells you when something worries them. With regular care at home, every session becomes shorter, cheaper and more pleasant for everyone.
