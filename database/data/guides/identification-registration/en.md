In Belgium, a dog without a microchip and registration is not legal. Above all, if it runs away, it has a much smaller chance of making it home. Identification has been compulsory since 1998, and the rules are now managed by the three regions with a single shared database: **DogID**. Whether you have just moved to Brussels for work or have lived in Ghent or Liège all your life, here is what you need to know.

## The principle: a chip and a registration

Identification has two inseparable steps:

1. **The microchip** (transponder), the size of a grain of rice, is implanted under the skin on the left side of the neck. It carries a unique 15-digit number. Only a **vet** may implant it.
2. **Registration** of that number in DogID, together with the contact details of the person responsible for the dog. The vet sends the data at the time of identification.

A chip on its own is useless if it is not linked to up-to-date contact details. Equally, an ID tag on the collar is a great extra, but it does not replace the chip.

## When must a dog be identified?

- **A puppy born in your home** that you keep must be identified and registered **before it is 8 weeks old**.
- **A dog that is sold or given away** must be identified and registered **before the transfer**, whatever its age. A puppy may not be sold or given away before 8 weeks.
- **A dog arriving from abroad** (moving house, adoption) must be registered in DogID. Book an appointment with a Belgian vet as soon as it arrives: they will read the existing chip and register it.

So when you buy or adopt a dog, it should already be chipped and registered. Be wary of any seller who offers to “do the chip later”.

> **Good to know**: since 2004, every dog identified in Belgium receives a **European pet passport** showing its chip number and vaccinations. Keep it safe and take it to the vet’s and on your travels.

## Change of owner, new address or death

The DogID data must always be up to date. That is your responsibility.

### New keeper

When a dog changes family, the registration must be transferred to the new keeper. DogID offers several ways to do this:

- **Online**, with both the previous and the new keeper, via a secure login (Belgian eID card or itsme).
- **With the official registration certificate**, completed and posted to DogID.
- **Through a vet**, who enters the transfer; the other party then has to confirm it.
- **Through the licensed breeder**, who can record the transfer at the time of sale.

After the transfer, you receive a new certificate in your name. Check it carefully.

### Moving house

Report your new address and phone number online on DogID or using the official form. If you move abroad, the dog must be recorded as “exported”.

### Death

Your dog’s death must also be reported to DogID, online, by e-mail, by phone or with the form. Your vet can help you with this.

## If your dog goes missing

1. **Report it immediately** as lost (or stolen) on DogID, online, by phone or through your vet.
2. **Make your contact details visible** in DogID, so that the vet, shelter or police officer who scans the chip can contact you directly.
3. Alert the shelters and vets in the area, the local police and your municipality.
4. Share a recent photo and the place where it went missing in your neighbourhood and in local groups.

Once your dog is home, remember to update its status in DogID.

## What changes from region to region

Since 2014, animal welfare has been a regional competence in Belgium. The basic identification rules are shared, but each region adds its own provisions.

### Wallonia: the ownership permit

Since 1 July 2022, anyone who wants to **acquire an animal** (buy, adopt or receive one) in Wallonia must show an **extract from the central register of environmental and animal welfare offences**, commonly called the “permis de détention”. It certifies that you are not banned from keeping animals.

- You request it from the municipal administration where you live.
- It is valid for 30 days.
- The seller, breeder or shelter must check it before handing over the animal.
- The rule applies to any acquisition **on Walloon territory**: if you live in Brussels or Flanders and adopt from a Walloon shelter or breeder, request the extract from the Public Service of Wallonia (allow up to 15 days for a reply).

The Walloon government has started a reform of the Walloon Animal Welfare Code, which plans to replace this extract with a **digital certificate** issued automatically. Until the reform applies, the procedure through your municipality remains the rule.

### Flanders

No prior permit is required, but the rules on selling are strict: a dog may only be traded if it is chipped, correctly registered, has a valid passport and is at least 8 weeks old. Adverts must always show the dog’s **microchip number**. The Flemish Animal Welfare department enforces these rules.

### Brussels

Brussels Environment is responsible for animal welfare. Identification and DogID registration are compulsory on the same principles, and breeders, boarding kennels and pet shops must be licensed.

## What is changing at EU level

In 2026, the European Union adopted a regulation on the welfare and traceability of dogs and cats. It will make microchipping and registration in interconnected national databases standard across the EU, phased in over several years. Belgium, which already requires the chip and DogID, is well prepared, but some details may change: keep an eye on official announcements.

> **Good to know**: do you also have a cat? It too must be identified and registered, in the **CatID** database.

## The “all in order” checklist

- My dog has a microchip implanted by a vet.
- It is registered in DogID, in my name, with my current address and phone number.
- I have its European pet passport and its registration certificate.
- My contact details are visible in case it gets lost.
- In Wallonia, I showed my extract from the municipality when I acquired it.
- I report any change (address, owner, death) without delay.
